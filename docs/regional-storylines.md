# PokéLife -- Regional Story Premises

The premises of the mainline and spin-off regional stories, the five-stage frame they follow, and the box legendary's part in each. The region code comments cite this file for the story titles. `docs/pokelife/MODULES/WORLD.md` owns the structure and contracts (every region open from day one, the journey advancing only through WORLD's own teams, the stories told as Pokémon-only adventures: 4.3, 14.6). The Mystery Dungeon arcs built are Rescue Team and Explorers (`docs/world-story-rewrite.md`). Unova, Kalos and Alola have their own stories in the code (`UnovaChapters`, `KalosChapters`, `AlolaChapters`).

## 1. Core Concept

Each Pokémon region is a self-contained adventure in which the player travels across the actual region, discovers locations, meets characters, investigates a regional conflict, and encounters the region's box legendary Pokémon.

### Design principles

-   Preserve the identity, geography, mythology, characters, villains, and legendary Pokémon of each source game.
-   Use recognizable towns, routes, forests, caves, mountains, islands, ruins, and legendary landmarks.
-   Make the box legendary central to the story from beginning to end, not merely a final boss.
-   Keep each region's story self-contained while allowing the player's overall PokéLife journey to continue across regions.
-   Do not force every region into identical characters, villains, dungeon types, or story beats.
-   Regional story completion must not require a full regional Pokédex or every side activity.

## 2. Regional Storyline Structure

Each region follows a consistent high-level framework, adapted to its
own canon.

1.  **Arrival** -- Introduce the region, starting settlement, local
    characters, expedition team, and central mystery.
2.  **Investigation** -- Explore nearby locations, meet allies, uncover
    clues, and investigate the antagonist or environmental threat.
3.  **Awakening** -- Reveal the box legendary's influence. Unlock
    important landmarks and discover the deeper regional conflict.
4.  **Confrontation** -- Complete a major expedition, confront the
    antagonist, and face the legendary Pokémon or the threat surrounding
    it.
5.  **Resolution** -- Restore regional balance, earn the legendary's
    recognition, and unlock regional completion rewards.

The chapter structure is a narrative framework, not a requirement that
every region use exactly five chapters or the same number of quests.

## 3. Box Legendary Integration

The box legendary should influence the region throughout the entire
storyline.

Suggested narrative progression:

1.  **Early game -- Signs:** Strange weather, unusual Pokémon behavior,
    environmental changes, dreams, sightings, or unexplained phenomena
    hint at the legendary.
2.  **Middle game -- Mythology:** The player discovers historical
    records, local stories, ruins, and clues explaining the legendary's
    role.
3.  **Late game -- Escalation:** The legendary's power becomes more
    visible, the regional conflict intensifies, and key locations are
    affected.
4.  **Final expedition -- Encounter:** The player reaches the legendary
    sanctuary and resolves the central conflict.
5.  **Postgame -- Recognition:** The player can pursue optional
    research, rematches, special encounters, collectibles, or further
    regional discoveries.

The legendary does not have to become a recruitable Pokémon immediately.

### Historical setting

-   **Hisui:** Can be treated as a historical Sinnoh expedition or a
    distinct time-period campaign. Its story can focus on the early
    relationship between humans and Pokémon, the Galaxy Expedition Team,
    regional nobles, and the space-time rift. Central legendary
    mythology: Dialga, Palkia, Giratina, and Arceus.
