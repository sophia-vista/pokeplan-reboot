# PokéLife -- Regional Expeditions & Main Storylines

## 1. Core Concept

Each Pokémon region is a self-contained adventure in which the player
travels across the actual region, discovers locations, meets characters,
completes quests, investigates a regional conflict, and encounters the
region's box legendary Pokémon.

The regional map is the backbone of the experience. The player should
feel like they are journeying through a Pokémon world---not selecting
disconnected quests from a menu.

### Design principles

-   Preserve the identity, geography, mythology, characters, villains,
    and legendary Pokémon of each source game.
-   Use recognizable towns, routes, forests, caves, mountains, islands,
    ruins, and legendary landmarks.
-   Make the box legendary central to the story from beginning to end,
    not merely a final boss.
-   Connect real-life PokéLife activities to travel, preparation,
    investigation, and exploration.
-   Keep progression generous. More activity should create more
    exploration, Pokémon encounters, and rewards.
-   Do not punish missed days, require perfect streaks, or make a
    specific life module mandatory.
-   Keep each region's story self-contained while allowing the player's
    overall PokéLife journey to continue across regions.

## 2. Regional Storyline Structure

Each region follows a consistent high-level framework, adapted to its
own canon.

1.  **Arrival** -- Introduce the region, starting settlement, local
    characters, expedition team, and central mystery.
2.  **Investigation** -- Explore nearby locations, meet allies, uncover
    clues, and investigate the antagonist or environmental threat.
3.  **Awakening** -- Reveal the box legendary's influence. Unlock
    important landmarks and discover the deeper regional conflict.
4.  **Confrontation** -- Complete a major expedition, confront the
    antagonist, and face the legendary Pokémon or the threat surrounding
    it.
5.  **Resolution** -- Restore regional balance, earn the legendary's
    recognition, and unlock regional completion rewards.

The chapter structure is a narrative framework, not a requirement that
every region use exactly five chapters or the same number of quests.

## 3. Regional Travel and Map Progression

### Map structure

Each region should have a navigable map based on its recognizable
in-game geography.

-   **Starting settlement:** Where the regional adventure begins.
-   **Routes:** Paths connecting settlements and exploration
    destinations.
-   **Settlements:** Towns and cities with characters, services, shops,
    and quest boards.
-   **Natural areas:** Forests, caves, mountains, oceans, islands, and
    other environments.
-   **Story landmarks:** Important locations tied to the regional
    conflict.
-   **Dungeons:** Multi-stage exploration destinations with encounters
    and rewards.
-   **Legendary sanctuary:** The climactic destination associated with
    the box legendary.

### Map behavior

-   Show the entire region as the player's world map.
-   Keep undiscovered areas obscured by fog of war or another discovery
    treatment.
-   Unlock routes and major destinations through story progression.
-   Let players revisit previously discovered locations.
-   Use optional paths and locations for extra exploration, Pokémon,
    items, lore, and Rare Candy.
-   Make settlements meaningful rest stops with characters, services,
    and local quests.
-   Use regional events and optional expeditions to keep completed areas
    relevant.
-   Show the player's current location and next available story
    destination clearly.

### Separate but connected progression tracks

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Track                               Purpose
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Story progression                   Unlocks major destinations and
                                      advances the regional narrative.

  Exploration progression             Reveals optional paths, landmarks,
                                      dungeons, collectibles, and
                                      encounters.

  Activity progression                Converts real-life PokéLife
                                      activity into travel, preparation,
                                      and expedition progress.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Story progression should guide the journey without forcing players to
complete every optional activity. Exploration should add depth and
rewards without blocking the main story.

## 4. Connecting PokéLife Modules to the Adventure

Use the existing module quest systems to generate expedition progress.
Avoid creating a completely separate daily quest tracker solely for
regional travel.

Examples:

-   **Fitness:** Traverse difficult terrain, climb mountains, cross
    routes, or prepare for physically demanding expeditions.
-   **Productivity:** Investigate an organization, organize supplies,
    complete research tasks, or help a settlement.
-   **Learning:** Decode inscriptions, study regional history, identify
    Pokémon, or solve clues.
-   **Personal / lifestyle modules:** Prepare provisions, help local
    characters, maintain the expedition camp, or complete everyday
    responsibilities.

### Integration rules

-   Multiple modules should be able to contribute to the same
    expedition.
-   Do not require a specific module for mandatory story progression.
-   Allow players to make progress through the modules they actually
    use.
-   Existing module quests can be framed as regional activities through
    quest text, characters, and rewards.
-   Story milestones should be achievable through normal, sustainable
    activity.
-   Missed days must not reset regional progress or create punitive
    catch-up requirements.
-   More activity should mean more exploration opportunities and
    rewards, not tighter resource scarcity.

## 5. Pokémon Encounters and Regional Content

Travel should produce meaningful discoveries.

-   Pokémon sightings can appear as random encounters, location-specific
    discoveries, or milestone rewards.
-   Different locations should have distinct encounter pools appropriate
    to their geography and source-game Pokédex.
-   Dungeons can contain multi-stage encounters, special items, and rare
    discoveries.
-   Optional exploration can reward Pokémon, collectibles, lore,
    currency, and Rare Candy.
-   Regional characters can offer side quests, recurring requests, and
    companion interactions.
-   Legendary encounters should be tied to story milestones, research,
    or optional postgame challenges.

## 6. Box Legendary Integration

The box legendary should influence the region throughout the entire
storyline.

Suggested narrative progression:

1.  **Early game -- Signs:** Strange weather, unusual Pokémon behavior,
    environmental changes, dreams, sightings, or unexplained phenomena
    hint at the legendary.
2.  **Middle game -- Mythology:** The player discovers historical
    records, local stories, ruins, and clues explaining the legendary's
    role.
3.  **Late game -- Escalation:** The legendary's power becomes more
    visible, the regional conflict intensifies, and key locations are
    affected.
4.  **Final expedition -- Encounter:** The player reaches the legendary
    sanctuary and resolves the central conflict.
5.  **Postgame -- Recognition:** The player can pursue optional
    research, rematches, special encounters, collectibles, or further
    regional discoveries.

The legendary does not have to become a recruitable Pokémon immediately.
Regional completion rewards can include recognition, a special companion
or encounter opportunity, a regional blessing, or a permanent passive
boost.

## 11. Regional Completion and Rewards

Completing the main regional storyline should provide a meaningful
permanent reward.

Potential rewards:

-   Regional guardian recognition or blessing.
-   Permanent boosts to relevant PokéLife modules.
-   New exploration destinations or postgame dungeons.
-   Special Pokémon encounters or recruitment opportunities.
-   Regional collectibles, cosmetics, decorations, and music.
-   Additional story content and legendary research.
-   Access to the next region or expedition campaign.

Optional exploration and collection completion should be separate from
main story completion. Players should be able to finish the regional
narrative without collecting every Pokémon or clearing every dungeon.

## 13. Key Implementation Requirements

1.  **Travel must be real progression.** The regional map cannot be only
    a decorative background or a quest-selection screen.
2.  **Use recognizable geography.** Destinations and route connections
    should reflect the source game's geography as closely as practical.
3.  **Keep story and exploration distinct.** Main story unlocks
    essential destinations; optional exploration reveals extra content
    and rewards.
4.  **Reuse the existing quest system.** Regional progression should
    integrate with existing module quests rather than duplicate them.
5.  **Support multiple activity paths.** No mandatory chapter should
    depend on one particular life module.
6.  **Make legendary presence continuous.** Foreshadow the box legendary
    early, develop its mythology through travel, and make its encounter
    the culmination of the story.
7.  **Make rewards generous.** Exploration should create more
    opportunities to acquire Pokémon, items, and Rare Candy.
8.  **Avoid punitive streak mechanics.** Missed days must not reset
    travel, story, or expedition progress.
9.  **Preserve regional identity.** Do not force every region into
    identical characters, villains, dungeon types, or story beats.
10. **Keep optional completion optional.** Regional story completion
    should not require a full regional Pokédex or every side activity.

## 14. Intended Player Experience

The player opens PokéLife and sees their character's current position on
a Pokémon-style regional map. They discover the next destination, accept
story and exploration quests, and complete real-life activities through
their existing PokéLife modules. Those activities contribute to
expedition progress, unlock routes, reveal locations, and generate
encounters. The player travels through the region, uncovers its story,
and eventually reaches the legendary Pokémon at the heart of the
regional conflict.

**The core loop:**

`Live your life → Complete module quests → Make expedition progress → Travel through the region → Discover Pokémon and places → Advance the story → Encounter the legendary → Earn regional rewards`

The result should feel like playing a Pokémon adventure alongside real
life, with regional travel and discovery serving as the connective
tissue between PokéLife's productivity and lifestyle modules.
